The human immune system protects the body against bacteria, viruses, fungi, and other harmful microorganisms. It consists of specialized cells, tissues, and organs that work together to recognize and eliminate pathogens. White blood cells, antibodies, and the lymphatic system play essential roles in immune defense. Vaccination strengthens immunity by training the immune system to recognize specific pathogens before natural exposure. Maintaining adequate nutrition, regular exercise, quality sleep, and stress management supports normal immune function.

Hypertension, commonly known as high blood pressure, is a chronic medical condition in which the force of blood against artery walls remains consistently elevated. Uncontrolled hypertension increases the risk of heart disease, stroke, kidney failure, and vision loss. Blood pressure is measured using systolic and diastolic values expressed in millimeters of mercury. Lifestyle modifications such as reducing sodium intake, maintaining a healthy body weight, regular physical activity, limiting alcohol consumption, and taking prescribed medications help control blood pressure.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus is a metabolic disorder characterized by insulin resistance and impaired glucose regulation. Patients may experience excessive thirst, frequent urination, fatigue, blurred vision, and slow wound healing. Long-term complications include cardiovascular disease, kidney damage, nerve damage, and diabetic retinopathy. Blood glucose monitoring, dietary management, physical activity, oral medications, and insulin therapy are important components of diabetes management.

Vaccines stimulate the body's immune response by introducing harmless components of infectious organisms. This process allows immune cells to develop memory without causing the disease itself. Immunization programs have significantly reduced illnesses such as measles, polio, diphtheria, and hepatitis. Some vaccines require multiple doses or booster shots to maintain long-term protection. Vaccine safety is continuously monitored through clinical studies and national surveillance systems.

Respiratory infections affect the lungs, airways, or upper respiratory tract and may be caused by viruses, bacteria, or fungi. Common symptoms include cough, fever, sore throat, nasal congestion, shortness of breath, and fatigue. Mild infections usually resolve with supportive care, while severe infections may require hospitalization and antimicrobial treatment. Hand hygiene, respiratory etiquette, vaccination, and avoiding close contact with infected individuals reduce transmission.

Cardiovascular disease refers to disorders affecting the heart and blood vessels. Coronary artery disease, heart failure, arrhythmias, and stroke are common cardiovascular conditions. Risk factors include smoking, obesity, diabetes, hypertension, elevated cholesterol, sedentary lifestyle, and family history. Early diagnosis through regular health screening and appropriate treatment significantly improves long-term outcomes. Lifestyle changes combined with medications can reduce disease progression.

Kidney function is essential for removing waste products, balancing electrolytes, regulating blood pressure, and maintaining fluid balance. Chronic kidney disease develops gradually and may remain asymptomatic during early stages. Diabetes and hypertension are leading causes of kidney damage worldwide. Routine blood and urine tests help assess kidney function. Patients with advanced disease may require dialysis or kidney transplantation.

Mental health includes emotional, psychological, and social well-being. Conditions such as anxiety disorders, depression, bipolar disorder, and schizophrenia affect millions of individuals globally. Early identification and timely treatment improve quality of life and functional outcomes. Treatment approaches may include psychotherapy, medication, lifestyle modifications, stress reduction, and community support. Mental health is an important component of overall health.

Antibiotics are medications used to treat bacterial infections by killing bacteria or preventing their growth. They are ineffective against viral infections such as the common cold or influenza. Inappropriate antibiotic use contributes to antimicrobial resistance, making infections more difficult to treat. Patients should complete prescribed antibiotic courses unless advised otherwise by healthcare professionals. Responsible antibiotic stewardship helps preserve treatment effectiveness.

Nutrition plays a critical role in maintaining health and preventing chronic diseases. A balanced diet includes fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean proteins, healthy fats, vitamins, minerals, and adequate hydration. Excessive intake of sugar, sodium, and saturated fats increases the risk of obesity, diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular disease. Healthy dietary habits combined with regular physical activity contribute to long-term wellness and disease prevention.
